Net value at every possible threshold

The threshold is a business decision, not a modeling parameter. The default 0.50 cutoff most models ship with leaves $1,150 on the table — and every cutoff below 0.13 or above 0.94 loses money outright.

Why 53% precision is the right answer

Roughly half of every call is "wasted" on a customer who was staying anyway. That is correct. A wasted call costs $100; a caught churner returns $350. A team optimizing for precision would feel more efficient and make less money.

What we can actually change

Inactive customers churn at nearly double the rate of engaged ones. Unlike age or geography, engagement is a lever the bank can pull.

⚑ Data quality: do not build a campaign on the 3–4 product segment yet

Customers holding 4 products churn at exactly 100% (60 customers) and 3 products at 82.7% — while 2 products churn at just 7.6%. A perfectly clean 100% in a small cell is a reason for suspicion, not celebration.

Three hypotheses worth testing before anyone acts on this: (a) these products were opened during complaint or problem-resolution flows, making product count a symptom of a customer already in trouble; (b) aggressive cross-selling into unsuitable products; or (c) a data-definition issue where a departing customer's products are counted at exit — which would make this feature leakage and require removing it from the model.

Data: public 10,000-customer retail banking churn dataset. Every figure on this page is computed from a live model — gradient boosting, 0.872 test ROC-AUC — scored on a held-out test set. Nothing here is illustrative.

Cost assumptions ($100 offer / $1,500 CLV / 30% save rate) are stated rather than hidden, and are the right thing to argue about with a retention team. The framework survives changing them; the specific threshold does not.

What I'd build next: an A/B holdout. Predicted churn is not the same as preventable churn — some high-risk customers are leaving no matter who calls. Contact a random half of the top deciles, leave the other half alone, and measure the difference.
